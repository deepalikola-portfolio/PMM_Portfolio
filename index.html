About

I'm a final-semester student at W.P. Carey School of Business, ASU, graduating May 2026 with a B.A. in Corporate Accounting and continuing into the MSBA program. I've spent the last two years building messaging matrices for 30+ AI and cloud solutions at the AWS Cloud Innovation Center, running go-to-market workflows end-to-end, and watching very smart technology sit unused because nobody built the bridge between capability and trust.

That's the thread running through everything I build. Not how to sell technology — how to make it feel safe to adopt. Whether it's an iPhone owner who hasn't turned on Apple Intelligence, a homeowner sitting on the fence about solar, or an enterprise team skeptical of AI infrastructure — the psychology of hesitation is the same. The marketing playbook shouldn't be.

I'm building a portfolio of three interactive projects, each targeting a different industry, to show that gap — and how to close it.

Approach

Three industries.
One marketing problem.

AI features, clean energy, and AI infrastructure each face the same adoption wall — people don't distrust the product, they distrust the change. My portfolio explores one marketing framework applied across three different trust gaps.

AI Consumer

Feature adoption hesitation

76% of eligible users haven't activated AI features on their devices. The technology is already in their pocket. The gap is psychological — awareness, privacy friction, or loss aversion. Each needs a different intervention.

Clean Energy

The clean energy trust gap

Consumers want sustainable energy but hesitate at the point of commitment. The marketing problem isn't generating interest — it's converting consideration into action when the stakes feel permanent and the technology still feels new.

AI Infrastructure

Enterprise AI skepticism

Enterprise buyers are more sceptical of AI infrastructure claims than any other category. The activation gap here is about proof — not demos, not case studies, but the specific signals that make an IT decision maker feel safe saying yes.
