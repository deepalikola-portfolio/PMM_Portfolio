Result Page Design

The result page is the tool's primary conversion surface. Its job is to make the user feel understood, reduce decision anxiety, and route them to one primary action. Three psychological principles govern the design: competence, control, and confidence.

Result Page Structure

BlockContent and Design Rationale
Stage TagLabeled badge (e.g., 'Stage 2 — Economics Skepticism'). Orange or green by severity. Validates the user's position without judgment.
HeadlineAssembled dynamically from housing + motivation + tech stack. Example: 'Your solar panels are exporting power at the wrong time. Here is what NEM 3.0 means for you.'
Energy Profile3-cell grid: State/Market, Technology Stack, Bill Level. Acts as a mirror — user sees their situation reflected back accurately.
Insight BlockOne key fact specific to their situation. Sourced from LBNL, CPUC, FERC, or EIA. Not a sales claim — a data point that reframes the decision.
Climate/Outage AlertShown only for FL/TX/CA users with resilience motivation. Shows local outage duration data.
Savings EstimatorStage 2 only. Single slider (monthly bill). Output: annual savings range, payback years, 30% ITC applied. Methodology expandable on demand.
Primary RecommendationAction box: dark background, large headline, single CTA button. Only one CTA. No competing links in this block.
Secondary OptionsTwo to three additional actions listed below the primary block. Lower visual weight.
What's Blocking YouFour-option micro-question: cost/complexity/relevance/partner buy-in. Each reveals a one-paragraph reframe without leaving the page.
Share/SaveCopy summary to clipboard. Open in email (pre-filled subject + body). Not shown until after the primary CTA zone.
The tool does not say what to do. It shows the user what is true about their situation, and lets the action follow logically from that.

Emotional Design Principles

Understood: The headline uses their specific tech stack and location — 'your solar panels' and 'your market,' not 'homeowners in your situation.'

Confident: Every stat is cited (LBNL, FERC, CPUC, Brattle Group). Methodology available on demand. Range outputs, not false precision.

In control: DR enrollment is labeled 'unenrollable anytime, override rights required.' Stage 3 friction is addressed directly before asking the user to act.

Not sold to: No email collection before showing results. Lead capture is optional and appears after the recommendation — not before.

07

UX Improvements

The v1 tool correctly identifies psychological stages and routes to relevant actions. The v2 redesign addresses five specific UX failure modes identified in the existing implementation.

7.1 — Decision Framing

Problem: Options presented as neutral choices. Users without energy knowledge cannot evaluate them.
Fix: Each option includes a one-line consequence note in gray text. Example: 'Homeowner — condo/HOA → some products may require board approval.' Consequence appears before selection, not after.

7.2 — Progress Cues

Problem: Progress bar shows question number but not estimated time or what is coming.
Fix: Add '2 min left' countdown. Add micro-label above each question: 'This tells us your upgrade path.' Users should understand what their answer does, not just that they answered.

7.3 — Results Page Hierarchy

Problem: v1 results are flat — all information at the same visual weight.
Fix: Three-zone hierarchy: (1) Mirror zone — situation reflected back. (2) Insight zone — one sourced key fact. (3) Action zone — one primary CTA, dark background, full width.

7.4 — Social Sharing

Problem: No sharing mechanism. The diagnostic produces insight users want to share with a partner.
Fix: (a) Copy summary — one paragraph with stage, housing, location, and primary recommendation. (b) Open in email — pre-filled subject 'Our energy situation — worth 2 minutes.'

7.5 — Partner Decision Support

Problem: Energy decisions are household decisions. The tool addresses one person.
Fix: After the primary CTA, add 'Share this with your household.' Pre-fills an email with the diagnosis and a single question: 'Does this match how you see our situation?'

7.6 — No Lead Capture Before Value

Email gate before results eliminates 40–60% of users before they see any value (HubSpot 2023). v2 principle: show the full diagnosis, then offer an optional save/email feature. Lead capture belongs after the savings estimator — not at the door.

8.2 — Lead Generation Funnel

Partner API: feed qualified leads (with consent) to Tesla Energy, Sunrun, Mosaic, or local installer network.

8.3 — VPP Enrollment Pipeline

Every user with solar + battery installed is VPP-eligible today. In production, this path connects directly to the Tesla Energy Plan API (CA) or Green Mountain Power (NE) for in-tool enrollment. DR enrollment (smart thermostat path) can be completed in-tool via utility API. Target: Nest + Ecobee integration to handle enrollment without redirect. VPP identity layer: 'You provided 3 hours of backup to your neighborhood last summer.' This transforms an economic transaction into community identity — the highest-retention mechanism available.

Tesla Energy Message Architecture

This section defines the message architecture for a hypothetical Tesla Energy deployment of GridGap. Message architecture is not copy — it is the strategic hierarchy that determines what is said, in what order, to whom, and why.

Brand Voice Principles

Precise, not reassuring. Tesla Energy does not tell customers 'you're doing great.' It tells them what the data says.

Advisor, not salesperson. No urgency language. No 'limited time.' No 'act now before prices rise.'

Every claim is sourced or sourceable. The architecture assumes a skeptical, research-oriented audience who will verify statistics.

Control language is mandatory for DR and VPP. 'You can unenroll anytime' and 'your override is always available' must appear before any enrollment CTA.

What the Tool Never Says

Never: 'Save up to X%' — range claims without methodology erode trust with Stage 2 audiences.

Never: 'Join thousands of homeowners' — social proof without specificity reads as marketing, not evidence.

Never: 'Limited time' or 'act before the incentive expires' — urgency language contradicts the advisor tone.

Never: 'Our products' or 'Tesla recommends' in diagnostic output — the tool is an advisor, not a storefront.

Never: Omit the AZ or NEM 3.0 disclosures to simplify output — a tool that tells users what they want to hear destroys trust when reality surfaces.

Conversion Lift Hypothesis

Five testable hypotheses about which message or design change would produce the largest lift in conversion rate at each funnel stage. Each is structured as a falsifiable claim with a proposed test design, primary metric, and expected effect size.

H1 · Savings Estimator Auto-Population

Auto-populating the savings estimator slider with the user's reported bill amount will increase estimator interaction rate by 25–40%.

Mechanism: Users presented with a pre-populated slider see a result immediately, creating curiosity that drives interaction rather than requiring instruction.

Test design: A/B test. Control: slider at $150/month. Treatment: slider pre-populated with user's Q3 answer. Primary metric: slider interaction rate. Sample size: ~1,200 Stage 2 result views per variant.

Expected lift: 30–40% increase in estimator interaction rate → 8–12% increase in quote request rate.

H2 · DR Enrollment Copy

Describing the DR program in plain language before using the term 'demand response' will increase DR enrollment CTA clicks by 35–50%.

Mechanism: 11 of 12 interview participants had never heard of demand response. The term creates a vocabulary barrier before the product value can be communicated.

Test design: A/B test. Control: 'Enroll in Demand Response.' Treatment: plain-language description first, 'demand response' as label only. Sample size: ~800 Stage 1 result views per variant.

Expected lift: 35–50% increase in CTA click rate. High confidence — 9 of 9 participants who received the plain-language description said they would enroll immediately.

H3 · Partner Share Feature at Stage 3

Adding a 'Share with your household' CTA with pre-filled email copy to the Stage 3 result page will increase 30-day quote request rate by 20–30%.

Mechanism: 7 of 12 Stage 3 participants cited partner buy-in as the primary blocker. The pre-filled email reduces the friction of composing an explanation from scratch.

Test design: A/B test. Control: Stage 3 result page without share feature. Treatment: with 'Share with your household' CTA. Primary metric: 30-day quote request rate. Sample size: ~600 Stage 3 completions per variant.

H4 · NEM 3.0 Plain-Language Alert

Replacing the technical NEM 3.0 description with a one-sentence plain-language summary followed by rate data will increase time-on-page for CA solar owners by 40%+ and reduce bounce rate by 15–20%.

Mechanism: P01 rated the original NEM 3.0 alert as 'confusing' and skipped the block entirely. A user who skips the alert does not understand why battery is the primary recommendation — so the CTA appears unmotivated.

Test design: A/B test targeting CA users who selected 'solar panels' in Q2. Sample size: ~400 CA solar owners per variant. Primary metric: time-on-page. Secondary metric: Powerwall CTA click rate.

H5 · Waiting Cost Table vs. Generic Deferral Message

Replacing the generic deferral message for Stage 3b users with a three-row waiting cost table will increase quote request rate by 40–60%.

Mechanism: Stage 3b users believe waiting is rational. A generic urgency message bounces off this frame. A cost-of-waiting calculation engages the same analytical process the user is already using to justify delay — but reverses its conclusion with specific numbers.

Test design: A/B test targeting Stage 3b completions only. Control: text message. Treatment: three-row comparison table (tech cost, ITC, utility rebates — each with 'wait' in red and 'act now' in green). Sample size: ~500 Stage 3b completions per variant.

Expected lift: 40–60% increase in quote request rate for Stage 3b segment.
