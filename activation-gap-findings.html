Adoption hesitation is not one thing. The 76% who have not activated are three distinct psychological states — each requiring a different message, a different proof point, and a different call to action.
Core insight
The three psychological stages
1
Passive Upgrader
No personal
relevance found
Has the device. Updated the OS. Never consciously connected a feature to a moment in their life. May already be using AI features without recognising them.
Show the outcome, not the feature. One job-to-be-done matched to their specific routine.
2
Active Evaluator
Unresolved trust
question
Has researched. Holds a privacy or data concern that Apple's documentation has not answered to their satisfaction. Knows what Apple Intelligence can do.
Acknowledge the concern before addressing it. On-device processing proof. Peer validation from skeptics who converted.
3
Decision Paralysis
All the information.
Still hasn't acted.
Loss aversion — the perceived cost of being wrong feels irreversible. Multiple open tabs, no decision. Information is not the gap.
Reversibility is the headline. One feature, not the suite. Give them the exit ramp before the on-ramp.
Key research findings
01
The label is the barrier, not the feature. 90% of users already use mobile AI — they just do not call it that. Naming Apple Intelligence as a system creates cognitive overhead that job-to-be-done framing removes entirely.
02
Stage 2 users are not uninformed — they are skeptical. Privacy concern intensity is high across all markets. 82% of gen-AI users worry AI could be misused. Avoiding the concern in messaging confirms their suspicion.
03
Choice overload drives Stage 3 deferral. Presenting the full Apple Intelligence feature set increases deferral. A single reversible feature — notification summaries, photo cleanup — with an explicit opt-out statement collapses the decision cost.
04
In-flow prompts outperform settings pages by 50–70%. Stage 1 users do not fail to find information — they never encounter the feature at the moment of need. Contextual activation at the job surface is the highest-leverage intervention.
The invisible AI problem
99%
of Americans use an AI-enabled product weekly
36%
say they used AI in the last week when asked directly
The 63-point gap between actual use and self-reported use means a significant portion of the 76% non-activation figure may already be using Apple Intelligence features — without identifying them as Apple Intelligence. The marketing problem is not just activation. It is attribution and identity formation around the feature set.
What the research says works — by stage
Stage What works What does not work Source
Stage 1
Passive Upgrader		 Concrete JTBD framing at the moment of need. 'Turn long texts into a 30-second summary' at the share sheet — not on a settings page. Contextual in-flow prompts improve activation 75–85% vs static pages. Leading with 'Apple Intelligence is now available.' Generic AI feature tours. Any headline that uses the word 'AI' before showing the outcome. Agile Growth Labs, 2025; Samsung Mobile Press, 2025
Stage 2
Active Evaluator		 Explicit acknowledgment of the data concern before any feature content. Private Cloud Compute named specifically — on-device processing is the strongest differentiator in category. Granular controls visible before CTA. Passive reassurance ('we take your privacy seriously'). Leading with features before addressing the trust question. Framing ChatGPT integration as a default. Deloitte, 2024–2025; Apple Newsroom, 2024
Stage 3
Decision Paralysis		 Reversibility as the headline. One feature only — not the suite. 'Turn it off in 10 seconds anytime' as primary message, not disclaimer. Micro-commitment framing: start here, add more later. Urgency. Multiple feature options. Any framing that implies the decision is permanent or difficult to undo. Complexity at the activation moment. Decision science / Renascence, 2025; Samsung Galaxy AI adoption data, 2025
